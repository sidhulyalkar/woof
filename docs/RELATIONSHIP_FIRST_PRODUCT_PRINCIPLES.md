# Relationship-first product principles

Woof exists to improve the owner-pet relationship in the world outside the app. Engagement is useful only when it makes that relationship easier, safer, clearer, or more enjoyable.

## The core loop

`notice -> choose -> do together -> read the response -> adapt -> remember -> make next time easier`

A screen earns its place when it helps the owner do at least one of these things:

- choose a useful shared action,
- notice what the pet is communicating,
- handle or reinforce more clearly,
- adapt difficulty or context,
- remember evidence that should improve a later decision.

## Four kinds of progress

Relationship progress is not identical to obedience progress. Keep these axes inspectable rather than collapsing them into one score:

1. **Pet skill and comfort**: what the pet can do comfortably in a specific context.
2. **Owner handling fluency**: timing, setup, reinforcement, prompt fading, environmental choices, and knowing when to simplify.
3. **Pair communication and recovery**: noticing opt-outs or tension, taking breaks, changing the setup, and recovering after a hard moment.
4. **Shared repertoire**: activities, environments, reinforcers, routines, and rituals the pair has learned reliably work well together.

A session can improve owner or pair mastery while pet-skill mastery stays flat. Correctly noticing tension and making the next repetition easier is positive evidence, not a consolation result.

## Product hierarchy

When Woof opens, the useful hierarchy is:

1. **What would be good for us now?**
2. **How do I do it well?**
3. **What did Woof learn from how that went?**

XP, collections, chapters, and celebrations may decorate this loop. They do not own it.

## Interaction rules

- Prefer one obvious recommended action over a dashboard of equal-weight choices.
- Keep real training sessions low-attention: one instruction or handler focus at a time, large reachable controls, and obvious easier/break/stop actions.
- A safe stop is a valid outcome.
- Do not reward repetition volume by itself.
- Do not use guilt copy for skipping, stopping, or choosing an easier option.
- Do not interpret one difficult day as permanent inability.
- Ask a microquestion only when its answer can materially change a later decision.
- Missing information stays unknown until evidence earns a stronger claim.
- Use uncertainty visibly rather than hiding it behind fake precision.
- Social interaction is not inherently better than comfortable distance.
- The owner should spend more attention on the pet than on operating Woof.

## Success criterion

The best outcome is not that the user spent a long time in Woof. It is that they leave the app knowing what to do together, notice something useful while doing it, and come back with better shared evidence for next time.
